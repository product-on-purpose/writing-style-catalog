## On the Checkout Rebuild, Fourteen Months On

The checkout moved over on June 13, and it held through that first peak weekend, and I have been trying since then to work out what I wanted to say to you about it. What I keep coming back to is how long it took.

For about fifteen years I thought the hard part of a project like this was the building. I thought that because every one I had worked on early in my career was remembered by its launch date, and nobody afterward could tell you who had slowed down, or when, or at what cost to them. It took me most of those years to see that I was remembering the wrong stretch. The building was the easy part to remember. The stretch worth remembering was the fourteen months in which two checkouts had to stay alive at once, and nobody outside this team could see anything change.

I will tell you what I saw, and I am only reporting it. In February, Marcus Teel found a cart-state mismatch that he could have filed as low severity, and the March launch moved back by three weeks, and Dani Rowe was the one who made that call while the schedule was pressing on everyone. In April, Jordan Osei rewrote the payment callback handler over a weekend when a smaller patch was sitting right there, and that pushed the May window by eleven days. On June 9, Sam Wickfield held the regression bar when every hour of delay felt very large. I have watched teams make the other choice in each of those places. Some of them shipped on time. I will not tell you that those teams were wrong, because a few of them were fine for years afterward, and I do not know why.

What I do know is narrower. The decommission is set for July 14, and the cart-abandonment numbers will not mean much until early July at the earliest, and I cannot tell you today what they will show. You have the sense right now that this was the largest thing you will do for a long while. That is true, and I am not going to argue you out of it. But in ten years it will be two or three sentences you say to someone new, about the weekend somebody rewrote a handler, and I think you will say them more quietly than you expect.

I have no advice to attach to this. Rest, if you can. The rest I would only be guessing at.
