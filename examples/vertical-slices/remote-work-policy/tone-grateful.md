To the people who objected to Position Brief v2 this month:

The office-first leaders who read the brief did not tell me it was fine. You told me that a calendar with no shared days leaves unplanned conversations to chance, and that I had not yet said what the anchor days were for. You spent your evenings on a document that was arguing against part of what you believe. Because of that, the brief now says that Tuesday and Thursday are coordination infrastructure, not a proximity mandate. The sentence is better than the one I started with, and it is yours.

To the colleagues who wrote back from outside commuting range, and the ones who wrote about caregiving and disability: you put real circumstances in front of me when you could have kept them private. You pointed out that a fixed obligation stated upfront is something a candidate can weigh, and that an unwritten five-day expectation is something they cannot. I had a principle. You gave me the reason behind it, and the response to the remote-only objection now rests on your reason.

To whoever raised the worry in Q&A that junior employees would lose mentorship density: I had no answer, and you asked anyway, in a room where it was easier to stay quiet. That question is why the Manager FAQ exists. It is due on June 27, and it covers how to onboard new hires under a hybrid model, because you made me see the gap.

I came into this week believing the position held on its own. It holds because each of you pushed on it.

Priya Ahluwalia
Policy Working Group Lead
Week ending June 20
