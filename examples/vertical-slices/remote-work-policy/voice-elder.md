## On Tuesdays and Thursdays

Priya, you asked me to read Position Brief v2 before the briefing on June 28, and I have, twice. I should say first that I am not the person to tell you whether it is right. What I can tell you is what I have watched happen to rules like this one.

For about fifteen years I believed that a team which sat together would, in the end, be a better team than one which did not. I believed it so firmly that I never checked it, and I was wrong about some of those years in ways I only saw later. The groups I thought were close were often just quiet, and a few of the ones I worried over were doing fine from three different cities. I was not wrong about everything. Some of the best work I saw was done by people who happened to be standing next to each other. But I held the belief as a fact when it was a preference, and I am telling you so that you know what you are getting when you ask me.

The first three times I saw an office leadership group announce a firm number of days, the number lasted about a year. Nobody defied it. It wore away, one understandable exception at a time, until two days meant whatever each manager remembered it to mean. The Tuesday and Thursday you have chosen will face that, and I am not saying it will happen to you. I am saying it is what happened to the ones I watched, and the brief you wrote already names that drift as the likeliest way this fails, which is more than I managed to do back then.

The people who relocated and arranged their childcare around what they were told when they were hired are holding something at full size, and it is full size. In ten years it may be two sentences in a story they tell about the year the policy changed. That is a different thing from telling them it is small this week, and I would not say it to them now.

As for the argument between the two camps, I do not know who is right, and I knew once and have stopped being sure. What I do know is that every person in those rooms was describing something that had actually happened to them, and the policies that lasted were the ones that said so plainly and let the disagreement stand.

I have no advice about July 3. I wanted to tell you that I read it carefully, and that I was glad it was written by someone willing to be wrong in public.
