Your return-to-office debate has been running for months, and it keeps landing on the same two bad options: require everyone back five days, or leave the location question open indefinitely. Neither survives contact with your actual constraints. A full return breaks promises you made to people who relocated and restructured their lives around flexibility. No mandate at all leaves the trust-and-coordination problem that leadership keeps raising unresolved, and it gets worse with every new hire who has never shared a room with a teammate.

You do not need a third debate. You need two anchor days.

The hybrid-anchor model replaces the binary with one fixed, protected block of shared time and a flexible default for everything else: two days a week - we use Tuesday and Thursday - when the whole team is expected on site, and three days where each person decides for themselves, no approval required either direction. That is the entire policy. It fits on an index card, which is the point: a policy your team can recite is a policy your team can actually follow.

Here is what two days buys you that zero days cannot. Spontaneous collisions - the hallway question, the whiteboard session that changes a decision - need predictable overlap, not daily proximity. Two reliable days a week gives you that overlap without asking anyone to give up three days of commute time they already rely on. And because the remaining three days carry no location requirement, you keep access to talent in markets where you have no office, which a five-day mandate forecloses outright.

This will not satisfy everyone on day one. People who structured their lives around full flexibility will feel two office days as a real cost, not a rounding error, and you should say that plainly rather than sell it as painless. Some candidates will still walk away from any mandatory presence requirement. That is a known trade, not a flaw you missed along the way.

Protect the anchor days from individual opt-outs for at least one quarter before you touch anything else. Opt-outs are how anchor days quietly stop being anchor days, and you will not know whether the model works until you have run it without exceptions long enough to see the collisions it was built to produce.

Pick your two anchor days this week.
