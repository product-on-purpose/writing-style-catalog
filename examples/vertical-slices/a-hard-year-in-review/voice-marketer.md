You have a message from someone who matters to you, and you read it the day it arrived, and you still haven't answered. Maybe that was April. You tell yourself you're giving it room, or waiting for the right words, or that a long silence has its own kind of mercy. None of that is what's happening. What's happening is that the longer it sits, the harder it is to open, and you already know that, which is why you keep not opening it. That's the alternative you're living with today: not deciding, dressed up as deciding later.

This year, a project I had led for eighteen months closed without shipping anything, and eleven people who gave it real time got a thank-you that did not match what they'd put in. I still haven't corrected that. Looking back at it alongside a six-year friendship that went quiet over the same stretch, the failure in both cases wasn't effort. It was the exact habit you're running right now: managing how something looked instead of saying the smaller, truer thing early, while it still cost less to say.

With the project, I kept a coalition optimistic when some of them needed a harder truth months sooner, and the silence only broke when the funder pulled out and there was nothing left to manage. With the friendship, I called the growing distance independence. It was avoidance. Avoidance compounds - eighteen months is roughly how long it took the project to fail outright, and that is not so different from how many months a message can sit unanswered. Both get more expensive the longer they run, not less.

Here is the part you can use without having lived through either loss: the fix was never a better message. It was a smaller, earlier one. You do not need to account for the last several months before you say anything at all. You need one sentence that is true today, nothing more finished than that.

Write it. Not a plan to write it this week, not a note to get to it soon. Send one message, now, to the person you've been avoiding. That's the only action here, and it's the one I didn't take soon enough to matter.
