I went into December expecting to list what I lost this year. When I sat down to write it, I found that the list I kept reaching for was of what other people gave me while I was losing it.

To the eleven people on the Meridian coalition: you gave eighteen months of unpaid evenings to a broadband proposal that the funder withdrew from in March. You kept showing up in the weeks when I was managing the mood of the group instead of telling you what I saw, and you kept the work technically sound anyway. The proposal was good because you made it good. None of it became a deployment, and you still gave it. I have not thanked you properly, and a short note would not have been the right size for what you did.

Celeste, for six years you were the person I called first. In the spring, when Meridian closed and I went quiet, you left the door open longer than I deserved. You asked how I was, and I answered with the version that kept you outside. That version was my doing, not yours. What you gave me over those years is the reason I knew what to be sorry about when the distance came. The friendship is not what it was, and I am still in your debt for what it was.

Theo, you wrote to me in April when you had no reason to think I would answer. I read it and did not reply. That message told me someone still thought I was worth writing to in the worst month of the year, and I carried it through the rest of the winter. I should have said so in April. I am saying it now.

I did not get through this year by being strong. I got through it because the people above had already given me more than I knew how to spend.
