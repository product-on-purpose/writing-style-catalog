## A year I am not finished with

For about fifteen years I believed that a project which ended badly had ended because I had not worked hard enough at it, and this year I watched that belief fail again in plain view. Meridian closed in March. The funder withdrew, eighteen months of work by eleven volunteers went undeployed, and the proposal that had been technically sound did not matter to the people who held the money. I have seen four or five efforts end this way over the years, and I can report that the first three times I went looking for my own mistake and found one every time, which is how I came to trust the search. It took me until this autumn to notice that the search always finds something, whether or not the something caused the ending.

I did make mistakes on Meridian. I kept the coalition cheerful when some of them needed the harder news earlier, and I thanked them less than they were owed. I am not offering those as a lesson. They are simply what happened, set down next to the funder's decision, and I still cannot say how much weight each one carried.

Celeste is harder, and I will be plain that I do not know what to say about her. We were close for six years. After Meridian the closeness thinned slowly and then quickly, and we have not spoken since August. In my experience, friendships of that length have come back about as often as they have not, and I have never been able to tell beforehand which kind I was in. I am not saying this one will come back. I am saying that I have been on both sides of that outcome, and that the silence feels final in a way I have learned not to trust, in either direction.

Next year, or in ten, this will be a few sentences in a story I tell about the year the work and the friend both went. It is not that size yet. It is still the size of a whole December, and I am letting it be that.

There is a retrospective owed to eleven people, and a message from Theo that I read in April and did not answer. Those I do know about, because the years have settled them: they get harder the longer they wait. About the rest I have no advice, for you or for myself, and I would sooner say so than invent some.
