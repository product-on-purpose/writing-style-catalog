# Tidemark, on the day it opens

Tidemark opens to the public on June 30. I am Marisol Veen, I lead product here, and I am writing this myself because I have been through enough launch days to know that the announcement people remember is rarely the one the team polished.

For about fifteen years I believed a launch was the moment a product became real. I held that belief through more launches than I like to count, and I was wrong about it for nearly all of them. The product became real afterward, in the months when somebody who had never heard of us opened it on a slow Tuesday and either came back or did not. The announcement was only the day I stopped being the one person who knew it existed.

Here is what Tidemark does, said as plainly as I can say it. A small team keeps its customer feedback in too many places: support tickets, chat threads, survey exports, notes from calls. Somebody reads all of it, copies it into a spreadsheet, and the team argues about priority in a meeting. Tidemark gathers the feedback from the tools you already use, groups the recurring themes, and gives you one ranked roadmap that you can share with a link.

Twenty-two small teams ran that whole loop with us before today, and none of them needed to file a support request. I am glad of that, and I would be misleading you if I said it tells me how the next year goes. In my experience the first twenty-two are the kindest people a product ever meets. They came to us already wanting it to work. I have watched that same early warmth carry a product for a season and then end, and I have watched it turn into something lasting. Both happened, and I could not have told you beforehand which one it would be.

The free plan has no cap on feedback volume for the first ninety days. That is an experiment, and I do not yet know what it will cost us. The team plan is $29 a month for up to fifteen seats, and there is a custom plan for organizations that need single sign-on and audit logs.

You can sign up now at tidemark.io, or book a twenty-minute walkthrough with one of us if you would rather look first. Nobody will call you afterward. Anyone writing about it can reach us at launch@tidemark.io, and there is no embargo.

On July 7 the early-access teams will sit down with us and tell us where the ranking got things wrong. I expect it did, in places, and I would rather hear it from them than learn it from silence.

I do not know what Tidemark will be in five years. I know what it is today, and that it was made carefully by people who wanted it to be useful.
