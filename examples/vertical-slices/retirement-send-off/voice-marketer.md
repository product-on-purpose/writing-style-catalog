"It's a small thing." "It's probably already in there." "I'm not sure it counts."

If that is what you thought when you saw the ask about Howard's runbook, this is for you.

Howard Thayer spent twenty-six years as an Operations Coordinator at Crestfield Group. His last day was Saturday, June 27. Before he left, he sat down for two sessions in May and June with Dana Reyes and Marcus Okonkwo to write down what he had carried in his head for two decades.

You can check what came out of those sessions. The runbook covers the vendor escalation paths. It covers four utility contacts that existed nowhere in the official system, because Howard kept them himself. It covers the sequence to follow when the automated alerts do not tell the full story.

What it does not cover is anything Howard taught you one-on-one that never came up in those two sessions. Two sessions could not hold twenty-six years, and the team's own status report says as much: his judgment in an ambiguous situation is not something the runbook captures.

That is where your small thing comes in. A vendor contact who actually calls back. A workaround he showed you once at your desk. The question he always asked before anyone touched anything. If it lives in your head and not in the document, the runbook does not have it.

You do not need to write it up well. A name and a number is enough. Two sentences about a workaround is enough. The runbook is a living document.

Your note also lands where it gets used. Marcus is running a gap analysis against the six most common incident types Howard handled, due August 15, and a specific contact or practice gives him something concrete to check. Dana's target is at least one incident closed on the on-call rotation through September 30 without escalating to Howard. Every entry you add is one less thing the person on call has to work out alone.

You do not have to decide whether it counts. Carolyn Marsh, the Operations Lead, is collecting them. Your part is to send it.

At his send-off on June 25, Howard spoke for four minutes. He said he was proud of the team and that he did not want to be the reason anyone felt stuck. Sending what he taught you is one way to make sure of that.

Send your vendor contact, practice, or workaround to Carolyn Marsh at cmarsh@crestfieldgroup.internal by July 11.
