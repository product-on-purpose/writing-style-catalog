Howard's last day is June 27, 2026. Before we say anything about what he meant to us, I want to work out what his leaving costs, starting only from things you can check against your own memory of the last twenty-six years.

**Premises.** Each one is yours to grant or refuse.

1. Howard has worked here for twenty-six years. This is an input taken from the personnel record, and you may check it.
2. Much of what Howard knew was never written down. When the old runbook stopped matching the system, he drew the real state of it from memory, and it was correct.
3. Knowledge that exists only in one person's head leaves when that person leaves.
4. Howard's mentoring was quiet. Several people here did not notice it happening to them until later.
5. A loss that nobody notices when it happens is a loss nobody plans to repair.
6. A team can only repair a loss it has named.

**Step 1, from premises 2 and 3.** The unwritten part of what Howard knew leaves with him on June 27. The files that do exist are not touched by his leaving, but the reasons behind them are.

**Step 2, from step 1.** So the first cost is not a missing document. It is a missing answer. The next time someone asks why the system works the way it does, the file will show what was done and will not show why.

**Step 3, from premises 4 and 5.** Howard's mentoring did not announce itself, so its absence will not announce itself either. Nobody will report that a question was not asked on their behalf, because nobody saw it being asked before.

**Step 4, from step 3 and premise 6.** The mentoring loss is therefore the one most likely to go unrepaired, because it is the one least likely to be named.

**Step 5, from steps 2 and 4.** The two costs differ in kind. The first one will show itself as a gap in an answer, and the team will feel it and can write it down. The second will show itself as nothing, and no one will feel it.

**Step 6, from step 5 and premise 6.** The only way to repair the second cost is to name it before it can be felt. A send-off is one of the few moments when a team gathers to name things on purpose.

**Conclusion.** What this team owes Howard on June 27 is two things. We should record the reasons behind the decisions he carried, while he is still here to give them. We should also say out loud, once, which people he mentored without being asked, so that someone owns the job of noticing whether they still get the attention he gave them.

**The load-bearing premise.** It is premise 4. Everything in steps 3 through 6 rests on Howard's mentoring being invisible while it happened. If it was visible, and the team already knows who he looked after and how, then the second cost is already named, the conclusion shrinks to the first half, and this send-off needs only the runbook. I do not think that is the case. Three people have told me separately that they did not see it until years later. If you know otherwise, that is the premise to push on.
