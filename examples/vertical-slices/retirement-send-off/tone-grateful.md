Howard,

When the system went down at midnight, you answered. I was new enough that I did not yet know which alarms mattered, and you stayed on the line until I did. That night is the reason I did not spend my first year afraid of the pager.

You were offered management more than once, and you said no each time, so you could stay where you understood the work. That choice cost you a title and a raise. What it gave the rest of us was a person who still remembered why a decision from eight years ago had been made, and who explained it to us accurately instead of leaving us to guess. On two occasions, that memory was the difference between a recoverable night and an unrecoverable one. I am grateful for both.

You never ran a mentoring program. You made time. One conversation with you, in my second month, changed what I thought I was allowed to attempt here. You probably do not remember it. I have thought about it more than you can know, and I have built a good part of my work on it.

Your last day is June 27. The team gathered for the send-off on June 25, and I wanted to put this in writing as well.

Thank you for staying on the line.
