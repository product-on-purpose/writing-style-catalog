Priya started on Monday, June 22, and Mei asked me to write down what I have seen happen to people in their first two weeks on this team, since I have been here longer than anyone else on it. I should say plainly that this is a report and not a plan. Mei has the plan, and it is a good one.

For about the first ten years I thought the first two weeks were about access. I believed a new engineer was a person waiting for credentials, and that if you got the repository, the VPN, the pipeline and the chat channels into their hands quickly, the rest would follow by itself. I was wrong about that for a long time, and nobody corrected me, because the people I was wrong about were polite and left before the pattern showed. What I learned, slowly, is that the access list is the part you can finish. It is finished on a Monday afternoon, as Priya's was, and the missing VPN certificate step gets written into the setup doc by evening, and then the real two weeks begin, and they have nothing to do with the list.

What I have watched, in somewhere past thirty people, is a quieter thing. A person arrives and spends the first days not wanting to interrupt anyone. They are blocked, and they do not say so, because everyone around them is shipping. We shipped four times this past week, and a newcomer can see that from the deployment log, and the sight of it makes asking for help feel expensive. Some of them learned to ask by Wednesday. Some of them took until the third week. I could not tell you in advance which would be which, and I stopped trying to guess around the year I stopped being sure of my guesses.

Here is what happened with the ones who shipped something of their own inside two weeks. They stayed. I am not saying that the shipping is what kept them, and I am not saying it will keep Priya. It may be only that the sort of person who ships in two weeks is also the sort who would have stayed. I do not know, and I have looked at it from both sides for a good while without settling it.

This week, Priya found her way to the test harness and the naming conventions by Wednesday, without anyone walking her there. On Thursday she sat in the design session for her first change, and she caught an edge case that the rest of us, who have all been here far longer, had walked past. I have been walking past things in that room for a long time. It is a particular feeling, being shown one by someone in her fourth day, and I would not call it embarrassing, only clarifying.

The first change is meant to be open by Wednesday, July 1, and merged by Friday, July 3, with Arjun pairing if she wants him. Staging access is sitting in the infrastructure queue, where it has been since June 23. It will probably be resolved. Requests like that one usually are, though I have seen them sit for three weeks, and the alert drill on July 2 will tell us whether this is one of those. I would not worry about it on her behalf. I would only note it.

Her first on-call week is in week five. That is a long way off from where she is now, and it will come as it came for everyone, and for most of us it was less than we had been afraid of and more than we had been told.

In ten years, I expect she will tell someone about her first two weeks, and the staging ticket will not be in the story. I do not know what will be. When I tell mine, it is a Tuesday and a man named Dennis, whom I have not seen in years, turned his monitor toward me and showed me how he had broken the build the day before. That is the whole story. It has never once been about the access list.

I do not have a recommendation for Mei's retrospective on July 3, except that she is going to ask Priya whether she feels she belongs, and I am glad of that. I do not know what Priya will say, and I do not know that the answer, whatever it is, will mean what we think it means after only two weeks. It takes longer than that to find out. It took me longer than that, by a good deal.
