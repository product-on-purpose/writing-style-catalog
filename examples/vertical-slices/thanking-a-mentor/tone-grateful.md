Dana,

Ten years ago you put my name forward to lead the Meridian rebuild. I had never run a project that size, and you knew it. You had your own team and your own commitments, and the safer choice was an established mid-level lead. You chose me anyway, and you put your own judgment on the line to do it.

You then stayed close through the first several months. You answered my questions, and you asked sharper ones back. You named the things I was not yet seeing. When I offered the work back to you in a moment of real panic, you did not take it. Taking it would have been faster, and it would have cost me what I most needed to learn. I did not understand then what that patience cost you. Nobody could have seen it on a delivery plan.

Because of that, I learned to carry something I had not carried before. I learned to make a call and live with it, and I have done that ever since.

Last month I put someone on my team forward for a project she did not feel ready for. I stayed close, and I held back when holding back was harder than stepping in. Somewhere around week four I realized I was doing exactly what you did for me. I had never been taught it in a course. I had been given it, by you.

I do not think you ever saw what that decision turned into. This is what it turned into.

Thank you, Dana.
