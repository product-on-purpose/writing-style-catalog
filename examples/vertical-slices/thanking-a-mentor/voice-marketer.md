Dana,

Ten years ago you had a decision in front of you, and the safe version of it was obvious: hand the platform migration to someone who already had a project that size on their record, not to the person who had just asked you not to put her forward yet. You had your own team to run and your own reputation riding on how it turned out. You made the other call anyway, and you put my name forward as lead before I had finished arguing that I wasn't ready.

Here is what that one decision bought, in plain terms. A six-month migration that shipped. A person who had never carried that much weight before, learning what it felt like to carry it. A decision pattern I still run today, a decade later, on the people I manage. None of that was guaranteed the day you signed off on it, and you knew that going in.

You did not make the bet and then step back to watch. You stayed close for the full six months - answering questions you had already answered once, sitting in the stakeholder meetings without taking the room away from me, and when I got something wrong in front of people whose opinion mattered, correcting me afterward instead of in the moment. You never once took the pen out of my hand, even in the weeks it would have been faster for you to.

Last month I ran the same play on someone on my team. I put her forward for a scope she did not think she was ready for, stayed close through the rough first stretch, and let her make calls I could have made faster myself. It is working. It is working because you ran this experiment on me first and absorbed the risk if it had gone the other way.

I do not think I ever told you how the migration landed, or what it turned into over the ten years since. That gap is mine to close, not yours to have wondered about.

So here is the one thing I am asking: let me take you to dinner, your choice of restaurant, before this turns into an eleven-year-old thank-you instead of a ten-year-old one.
