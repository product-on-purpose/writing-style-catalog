## On keeping a day of rest

For about eleven years I believed that a person who stopped working on a given day was a person who had decided the work was not that important, and I arranged my weeks around not being that person. I was wrong about this for a long time, and I do not have a tidy account of how I came to see it. It happened slowly, mostly by watching what the weeks looked like when I did not stop.

The first time I tried a rest day it lasted six weeks. A deadline pulled it under, and I told myself I would begin again when things settled. They did not settle, because they never do, and I began again eleven months later. That was two weeks ago. I am telling you the length of the gap because I think the gap is the more honest part of the story than the two weeks.

This past week, ending Sunday, June 21, I put the phone in a drawer on Saturday at eight in the evening and took it out at six the next morning. Ten hours. The week before it had been four. One message from work sat unanswered from Sunday afternoon until Monday, and I did not compose a reply in my head while it waited, which has not happened before. On Monday two problems I had been circling for days came apart more easily than they had any right to. I cannot tell you why. I am reporting what happened, and I am not saying it will happen to you.

There is still a small clerk in me who tallies whether the rest was worth what it cost. In my experience the clerk does not leave because you argue with him, and I have argued with him for years. What I can say is that he was quieter this week than he was two weeks ago, and that two weeks is a short time to hold against a habit that old.

In ten years this will probably be a sentence or two in some longer story about how I came to work the way I do. I do not know which sentence. I do not know whether the practice will hold through the next deadline, which is the thing that ended it the first time, and I will not pretend I have learned to be certain of that. It is very old, this practice, and older than I am. I am only now finding out what it was for.
