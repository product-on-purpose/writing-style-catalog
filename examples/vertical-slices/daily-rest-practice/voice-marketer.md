You measure your days by what they produced, and lately the measurement keeps coming back worse. Decisions take longer than they used to. You catch yourself redoing analysis you already did once. By the end of a long work stretch, your patience for a hard problem is thinner than it was at the start. None of that is a mood. It is a pattern, visible if you compare one week against the next.

The thing that has actually held, after a few failed starts, is one full day a week with no work output, no task completion, no checking messages. Not a lighter day. A different kind of day.

The install is a single decision, made the night before: close the laptop, put the phone in another room, and do not open either until morning. No app, no setting, no automation. The whole point is that you cannot schedule the willingness to stop.

The first attempt will not feel like rest. The first version of this practice ran six weeks before a deadline pulled it under, and the restart came eleven months later. It has held for fourteen weeks since. The phone-away window started at four hours and is now ten. Progress here is measured in hours added, not in a switch that flips.

Here is the cost, stated plainly: the feeling of control. The pull to check one more thing does not fade on the first few tries, and the early hours of the day can run anxious instead of calm, especially with something unresolved waiting. That discomfort is the cost. It is not a sign the practice is failing.

Here is what it has returned so far: two problems that had been stuck for days both cleared by the following Monday. That is one data point from one week, not a promise that it repeats for you. What does repeat is the shape of the week itself. Six days carry a different quality of attention once the seventh is genuinely off.

You do not need a system to start, and you do not need to wait for a lighter week, because there is no such thing as one. You need one evening.

Tonight, put your phone in a different room before you go to sleep, and do not open it until morning.
