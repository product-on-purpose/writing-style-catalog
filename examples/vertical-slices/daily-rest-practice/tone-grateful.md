To the colleague whose message sat unanswered from Sunday afternoon until Monday morning, in the week ending June 21: you did not follow it with a second message, and you did not ask whether I had seen it. You waited. I never told you what that did, so I am telling you now.

It was the first time I had let a work message wait through a rest day without drafting the reply in my head. I could only do that because nothing arrived behind yours to prove the silence was a mistake. Your patience cost you a morning of not knowing, and I did not earn it. It is the reason that Sunday counted as a rest day and not another workday spent half-watching the phone.

I owe a second debt to the person who noticed the gap in my availability a few weeks earlier and asked about it without suspicion. My explanation took more words than it should have, and you listened to all of them. That question made me say aloud that I was keeping one day in seven, and a practice I have said aloud is harder for me to quietly abandon. I had tried this twice before and both attempts collapsed, so that mattered more than you could have known.

Two problems I had circled for days came apart on Monday. I cannot trace how. I do know that the Sunday they rested behind was one that two people made easier to keep, and I did not ask either of them to.

Thank you.
