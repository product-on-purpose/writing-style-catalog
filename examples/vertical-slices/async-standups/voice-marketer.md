You've sat through the 9am Pacific standup when it was 9:30 at night where you live, said your three sentences, and logged off wondering why that couldn't have been a message. If you're on the India side of this team, you already know the number: you made it to 3.2 of 5 standups last quarter, against 4.6 for engineers on Pacific or Eastern time. That's not a discipline problem. That's a meeting that only works for people who aren't you.

Here's the part that holds even if you're on the convenient side of the clock: the standup runs 14 minutes and produces about 4 of signal - a blocker raised, a dependency flagged, something that changed what someone did next. The other 10 minutes is status nobody needed to hear live. And none of it sticks. One blocker that a previous standup had already resolved still cost the team 45 minutes before anyone realized it had been answered, because nothing from that call was searchable five minutes after it ended.

#team-standup replaces the call with one Slack post, due by 10am your local time: what shipped, what's in progress, what's blocked and who can unblock it. No call, no 9:30pm login, no waiting for your turn to talk. Post it, and it's there - searchable, @mentionable, read by the on-call engineer between 10 and 11am Pacific and answered within 30 minutes if you're blocked.

You get back roughly 70 minutes a week that used to go to standup. You don't get all of it back for free: the daily call is replaced by one 60-minute Thursday working session, not five short ones, and you'll see your team live less often than you do now. That's a real trade, not a hidden one. What you get instead is a channel that doesn't forget what you told it and doesn't make you choose between sleep and attendance.

This is a 30-day trial, not a permanent switch. The team reviews attendance, blocker resolution time, and how it actually felt at day 30, and reverts if it doesn't hold up.

Post your first update to #team-standup by 10am tomorrow, your time.
