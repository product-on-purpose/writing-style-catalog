For most of my adult life I woke at 6:30, reached for my phone before my feet found the floor, and called that a morning. I thought of it as a small failing of discipline that a good enough system would fix, and for about ten years I went looking for the system.

In May of 2026 I wrote down a plan, dated it the fourteenth, and gave it a review date of the fourteenth of June, because the three attempts before it had each lasted four to six days and I did not trust myself to remember why. The plan was water, light, movement, and ten minutes with a paper notebook, with the phone left in the kitchen until half past seven. It was not clever. People have been doing something close to it for a very long time, and I knew that when I wrote it.

I can tell you how the first month went, and I am telling you only that, not what it means for you. I finished 23 mornings out of 30. I held the 6:15 wake on 19 of them. Tuesdays were bad, for reasons I guessed at and never settled. Two Mondays I picked up the phone early. Earlier I had tried to stretch the thing to ninety days and quit on the eleventh, and I spent a while afterward blaming myself, which I now think was a waste of a few weeks.

What I got wrong for the longest time was the idea that the routine was the point. I believed that if I found the right hour, the rest of the day would follow it into order. It did not work like that. The hour only gave me somewhere to stand for a few minutes before the day arrived, and some mornings that was enough, and some mornings the cold or the travel or Monday's leftover load took it from me anyway.

Whatever it is you are trying to build, I would hold it at the size it feels, because a bad week at the start is a real thing and I will not tell you otherwise. I would only say that a missed Tuesday looks different from the far side. In a year, I suspect, nobody will be able to tell you which weeks you kept the plan and which you did not. They will remember whether the house was quiet at that hour, and whether you were in it.

I do not know whether the routine will hold for you. I do not know yet whether it will hold for me past the second month, and I decided not to pretend to. I can only report that I am still getting up, most days, to a glass of water that I set out the night before.
