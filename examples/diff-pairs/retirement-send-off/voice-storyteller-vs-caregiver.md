# Diff Pair: Voice swap - `storyteller` vs `caregiver`

**Topic:** Marking a long-serving colleague's departure
**Axis varied:** voice
**A:** `storyteller` - A voice that carries meaning through character, scene, and concrete moment, trusting the reader to extract the principle from the specifics.

## A: `storyteller`

There is a corner of the third floor where the fluorescent light has flickered since the renovation. Howard taped a piece of yellow legal-pad paper to the wall beneath it in 2019, wrote "maintenance ticket #4,218" and drew a small box around the number, as if marking it for later. The light still flickers. The note is still there.

Nobody asked him to file the ticket. Nobody noticed when he did. That was mostly how it went with Howard.

The Tuesday after the Graymont account nearly collapsed - the routing error, the three clients threatening to walk - Marta found him at six-forty in the morning already at his desk, a mug of tea steaming beside his keyboard, a spreadsheet open on his second monitor.

"How did you even know?" she said.

He looked up. "Lydia sent me something last night. I figured."

He had pulled the records back eight quarters. Not because anyone asked. Because that was the thing that needed to happen, and he was the person who knew where the records were.

The account didn't collapse. Marta got the call from the client. She always said Howard got no credit for that one, and Howard always said that wasn't the point.

"The point," he told her once, stirring his tea, not looking up, "is that the client stayed."

Twenty-six years is long enough to become part of the building. Long enough that new people ask him where things are before they check the directory. Long enough that when he is gone, the directory will be all that's left, and it won't know what he knew.

He leaves on a Thursday. His desk will be clean before anyone arrives Friday morning. That is also, in its way, exactly like him.

## B: `caregiver`

Howard is going to leave, and that is the thing we need to say first.

Not "Howard is retiring" in the way that sounds like a ceremony. Howard is going to stop coming in. His desk will be empty on a Monday morning when something goes wrong - and something will go wrong - and the instinct to walk to the end of the hall will not be rewarded anymore. That is what this is.

He spent twenty-six years here. Most of it at the same desk, in the same role, and that was not inertia. That was a choice. He understood that the most useful thing you can do in a place is stay long enough to become the memory of it. He was the person you called when you could not remember why the old system worked the way it did, or when you needed to know who to trust in an unfamiliar part of the building, or when you just needed someone to sit with you while the crisis ran its course.

He mentored people without naming it that. He answered questions at the end of the day when it was inconvenient. He noticed when someone went quiet in a meeting and found them afterward. He never said "I'm proud of you" because he did not need to. You knew.

Several of you are in careers that exist because of what Howard did quietly, and without fanfare, and without asking for anything back.

It will not be easy to fill that. It probably will not be filled, exactly - it will be carried differently, by more people. That part is going to take some time.

Today we are just here to be with him for a moment. To say: we saw you, even when you were not looking. And we are grateful.
