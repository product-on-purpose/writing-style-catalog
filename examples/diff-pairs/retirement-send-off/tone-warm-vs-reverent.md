# Diff Pair: Tone swap - `warm` vs `reverent`

**Topic:** Marking a long-serving colleague's departure
**Axis varied:** tone
**A:** `warm` - Communicates genuine care and personal regard for the reader - not flattery, but authentic human warmth.
**B:** `reverent` - Approaches the subject with deliberate care and weightiness - the tone of someone in the presence of something that matters.

## What to notice

Both honour twenty-six years, and they aim their attention at different objects.

**A speaks to Howard.** "Howard, you have been here longer than most people have known where
they wanted to work. Twenty-six years in the same role is not something that happens by
accident." Second person, and the compliment is built from his own life.

**B speaks about a kind of person.** "There is a kind of person who does not accumulate titles,
who does not chase the next role or the higher office, and whose value to an organization is
precisely and entir[ely]" - the particular man arrives only after the type has been established.

**The sharpest single tell is who the first sentence addresses.** Warmth begins with "Howard,".
Reverence begins with "There is a kind of person". One is a message to him; the other is a claim
about what he represents, which is why it can be read aloud to a room that includes him without
anyone being embarrassed.

## A: `warm`

Howard, you have been here longer than most people have known where they wanted to work.

Twenty-six years in the same role is not something that happens by accident. You chose it, year after year, because the work was worth doing and because the people doing it needed someone steady. That was you. When the system broke at the wrong hour or the deadline moved without warning, people did not panic - they found you. Not because you always had the answer, but because you made the problem feel survivable.

What most of the people you helped will not realize until later is how much of what they know, you gave them. You did not call it mentoring. You called it lunch, or a quick question, or "let me show you how this works." A dozen careers carry a piece of your patience in them, whether or not they know to name it.

We have been lucky to work alongside you. That is not a thing we say at retirement parties to be polite. It is specific: lucky that you stayed when staying was not glamorous, lucky that you cared about the right things at the right moments, lucky that you noticed the people around you well enough to know what they needed before they asked.

You are leaving the kind of absence that does not fill. We will keep your habits where we can. And we are glad - genuinely glad - that you spent this part of your life with us.

## B: `reverent`

There is a kind of person who does not accumulate titles, who does not chase the next role or the higher office, and whose value to an organization is precisely and entirely what they chose not to do with their ambition. Howard is that person. He spent twenty-six years in this building, most of them in the same chair, and what he built there was not a career ladder but something closer to a foundation.

He knew where the bodies were buried, as the saying goes, but that is too cynical a way to put it. He knew where the decisions came from. He knew which agreements had been made in which hallway in which year, and why they still mattered, and what would break if you forgot them. When systems failed and schedules collapsed and the pressure rose past what anyone had planned for, the people in that room looked for Howard. They always found him there.

He mentored without calling it mentoring. There are people at this company today whose paths through it were quietly shaped by a question he asked, a call he made, an afternoon he gave when he did not have to. He did not mark these things. He would not have thought to.

What leaves with him is not a set of tasks. What leaves with him is a kind of steadiness, a quality of presence that made the surrounding work feel grounded, that made this a place where something was expected to be done well, not merely done. There is no replacement in progress. There is no process document that captures what he carried.

We mark his departure because some things deserve to be marked.
