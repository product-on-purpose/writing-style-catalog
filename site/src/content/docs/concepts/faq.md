title: Questions and Objections
description: Straight answers to the questions people ask first, including the skeptical ones.

These are the questions people ask when they first see the catalog. Several of them are objections, and they deserve direct answers.

## Isn't this just a prompt library?

No. A prompt library is a list of finished prompts, one per task, and you pick one. This catalog is a set of named parts on four independent axes: Voice, Tone, Style, and Format. You pick one value on each axis, and the builder assembles them into a single instruction.

Three things follow from that design, and a prompt list has none of them:

- **Any combination works.** Pair the `pragmatic-architect` voice with a `warm` tone and an `adr` format, or swap any one of the four. The parts are written to be independent.
- **Clashes get flagged.** When one selected entry lists another in `avoid_with`, the builder warns you and orders the blocks by a fixed precedence. It never refuses a combination ([ADR 0016](https://github.com/product-on-purpose/writing-style-catalog/blob/main/docs/internal/adr/0016-conflict-aware-composition.md)).
- **Each part shows its effect.** Diff-pairs hold a topic and three axes constant and change one, so you can read exactly what that one choice did.

The [comparison page](../how-it-compares/) puts this side by side with prompt libraries, style guides, and tone presets, including where those are the better tool.

## Are the examples written by an LLM?

Yes, all of them. Every worked sample says so in its frontmatter with `author_type: llm`, and each one records the model that wrote it in `llm_model`. The policy for this is [ADR 0007](https://github.com/product-on-purpose/writing-style-catalog/blob/main/docs/internal/adr/0007-llm-example-author-policy.md), written before the first sample was generated.

LLM-written samples are the right evidence for this particular catalog. The catalog's job is to steer a model. A sample shows what a model actually produced from the instruction, which is what you will get when you use it. A human-written sample would show what a skilled writer can do, and that is a different claim.

The known risk is sameness. Samples from one model family tend to share habits across voices. ADR 0007 names that risk directly, and the blind tests on the [evidence page](../adherence-evidence/) are the check against it.

Two limits should be stated plainly:

- The samples carry `review_status: reviewed`. On worked samples, that status is set by the generation tooling once the automated checks pass. It does not mean a person read each sample.
- ADR 0007 also asks each sample to record the prompt file that produced it. The samples record the model but not the prompt file.

## Were the entries written by an LLM too?

The entries were drafted with LLM help and shaped through repeated review passes and automated checks. They were not hand-written by domain experts, and the catalog does not claim that they were.

What the catalog does claim is narrower and testable: the named distinctions steer a model's output in ways a blind judge can tell apart. That claim rests on measurement, not on who typed the words.

## How do you know it works?

It was measured three ways, all blind, and the [evidence page](../adherence-evidence/) records each run in full:

- **8 of 8** near-neighbour pairs were attributed correctly by a blind judge from the same model family. Chance is about 0.4 percent.
- **18 of 18** samples were attributed correctly by a blind judge from a different vendor.
- **3 of 3** deliberate caricatures were rejected, each for the failure mode its own entry documents.

The third result matters most. It shows the judge says no when it should, which is what makes the first two results count.

The limits are real, and the evidence page lists them. Each test covered between nine and sixteen entries, not the whole catalog. The judges saw short descriptions of each option. Every judge was a model, not a person. Every sample was generated by a capable model, so nothing yet shows that a smaller model follows the same distinctions. Treat the results as strong evidence that the premise holds, not as a published evaluation.

## What does "machine-verified" mean? Has anyone read these entries?

Every shipped entry shows its status on its page. Most carry `machine-verified`, which means two things:

1. The entry passes every automated check. It matches the frozen schema, its cross-references resolve, it carries the required `tells`, `anti_patterns`, and `failure_modes`, and it renders on all twelve anchor topics.
2. The maintainer has not yet read it line by line.

When the maintainer reads an entry and approves it, it moves to `stable`. Only the maintainer can make that move, one named entry at a time. Entries that have not cleared the checks stay `draft` and are not shipped. The reasons for this ladder are in [ADR 0021](https://github.com/product-on-purpose/writing-style-catalog/blob/main/docs/internal/adr/0021-honest-middle-review-status.md).

## Does it lock me into Claude?

No. The builder's output is a plain-text prompt prefix. You can paste it into the system prompt of any model, or put it in front of your request.

The Claude Code plugin is the most convenient way to compose instructions, and a ZIP also works in Claude.ai and Claude Desktop. The catalog content itself is licensed CC-BY-4.0, so you can use it anywhere with attribution.

One caution: the blind tests generated samples with Claude models. Nothing yet measures how closely other vendors' models follow the same instructions.

## Is it ready for production use?

Not yet, and the README says so. The project is pre-1.0. The entry schema is frozen, so the shape of an entry will not change without a major version ([ADR 0019](https://github.com/product-on-purpose/writing-style-catalog/blob/main/docs/internal/adr/0019-schema-freeze-and-change-policy.md)). The wording of entries, the skill interfaces, and the docs can still change.

It is usable today. Treat it the way you would treat any pre-1.0 tool: useful, and still settling.

## Why four axes? Aren't voice and tone the same thing?

They are the pair people confuse most, which is why the catalog separates them. **Voice is what does not change. Tone is what changes per piece.** A `pragmatic-architect` reasons from constraints and trade-offs in every document. The same voice can deliver a `candid` message one day and a `warm` one the next.

Brand writing often treats "voice and tone" as one phrase. Here you choose them as two separate parameters. The [four-axis model](../four-axis-model/) explains each axis and the test it has to pass.

## Can I use my own voice, or my company's?

Partly. The `style-profile` skill saves your preferred combination as a reusable default. It selects from the catalog's existing entries. It does not learn a new voice from your writing.

To add a voice the catalog lacks, you can propose an entry. The [contribution process](../../governance/contribution-process/) explains the bar a new entry has to meet. Because the content is CC-BY-4.0, you can also adapt entries privately for your own team.

## What does it cost?

Nothing. The code is Apache-2.0 and the content is CC-BY-4.0. There is no paid tier, no account, and no usage tracking in the plugin.
